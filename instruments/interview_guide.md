# Interview Guide

## Participants

Use this semi-structured guide with teachers, ICT trainers, cybersecurity professionals, local-government officials, civil-society representatives, community leaders, and relevant public-sector stakeholders.

## Opening

Explain the study purpose, voluntary participation, privacy limits, recording arrangements if applicable, and the right to skip any question. Obtain consent before proceeding.

## Core questions

1. How do people in this community currently use digital and AI-enabled tools?
2. Which groups benefit most from digital services, and which groups face the greatest barriers?
3. What examples of AI-related misinformation, fraud, privacy risks, or unfair automated decisions are most relevant locally?
4. What practical skills should a public AI-literacy programme prioritise?
5. How should training work for people with low literacy, limited data, disability-related access needs, or a preference for local languages?
6. What would make people trust or distrust AI learning tools?
7. What role should schools, libraries, faith communities, market associations, civil society, and government play?
8. How should a programme teach verification without suggesting that people can always detect synthetic media by appearance alone?
9. What safeguards are needed when AI is used in education, public services, finance, or employment?
10. What would count as meaningful improvement after a six-week training programme?

## Probes

Ask for concrete examples, differences by community or demographic group, suggestions for accessible delivery, and recommendations for policy or implementation.

## Closing

Ask whether there is anything important not covered and whether the participant can suggest relevant organisations, materials, or community channels.
