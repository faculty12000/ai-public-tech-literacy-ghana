# Research Protocol

## Status

This repository implements the study package for the canonical preprint, *Evaluating AI's Role in Public Technology Literacy in Ghana: A Mixed-Methods Research Protocol and Conceptual Framework*, by Fahad Mohammed Gibrine. It is a protocol and conceptual framework, not a report of participant results. Before recruitment, finalise ethics review, sample-size justification, allocation procedures, preregistration, and pilot testing.

## Title

Evaluating Artificial Intelligence and Public Technology Literacy in Ghana

## Background

Ghana's mobile-first digital adoption has expanded access to services and information, but use of technology does not necessarily indicate the ability to judge AI output, protect personal information, recognise scams, or seek human review of an automated decision. This study evaluates whether AI-supported learning builds these capabilities more effectively than conventional digital-literacy training.

## Aim

To evaluate the role of artificial intelligence in improving public technology literacy in Ghana, with attention to access, practical skills, critical thinking, safety, inclusion, and responsible use.

## Objectives

1. Assess awareness and use of AI-enabled tools.
2. Measure change in practical digital skills after AI-supported learning.
3. Examine change in information verification and critical evaluation of AI output.
4. Identify barriers related to location, income, gender, age, disability, education, device ownership, data cost, and language.
5. Assess understanding of privacy, cybersecurity, bias, misinformation, and responsible AI use.
6. Compare AI-supported training with standard digital-literacy training.
7. Develop recommendations for government, education providers, civil society, communities, and technology providers.

## Research questions

1. What awareness of AI and AI-enabled services do Ghanaian citizens have?
2. Which groups are most likely to use AI tools and which are excluded?
3. Does AI-supported learning improve performance on practical digital tasks?
4. Does it improve information verification and media-literacy behaviour?
5. Can participants recognise AI-enabled scams, phishing, privacy risks, synthetic media, and biased output?
6. Which forms of AI learning support are effective and locally appropriate?

## Study design

Use a mixed-methods quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test design. The intervention group receives six weeks of AI-supported digital-literacy training. The comparison group receives standard digital-literacy training without AI tools, or receives the intervention after the study. Collect baseline and endline data from both groups.

## Intervention

| Week | Topic | Practical outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Digital foundations | Device basics, internet navigation, accounts, and security |
| 2 | Understanding AI | What AI is, where it is used, and its limitations |
| 3 | AI for learning and work | Guided use for research, translation, CVs, and communication |
| 4 | Information verification | Comparing AI outputs with credible independent sources |
| 5 | Privacy and cybersecurity | Phishing, unsafe links, fraud, and risky data sharing |
| 6 | Responsible AI use | Bias, fairness, consent, intellectual property, and community impact |

Delivery should be practical, mobile-first, low-data where possible, locally relevant, and supported by local-language explanation and peer support. Suitable settings include schools, libraries, faith communities, youth centres, market associations, and district assemblies.

## Data collection

- Baseline and endline survey.
- Practical skills assessment.
- Focus groups with participant groups where appropriate.
- Semi-structured interviews with teachers, ICT trainers, cybersecurity professionals, community leaders, civil-society representatives, and relevant public officials.
- Structured observation of training sessions.

## Primary outcomes

- Change in practical-skills score.
- Change in ability to verify an AI-generated claim using an independent credible source.
- Change in phishing and unsafe-data-sharing recognition.
- Change in critical AI-literacy score.

## Equity outcomes

Report outcomes by location, age, gender, disability, education, income proxy, device access, data affordability, and language preference. Examine whether the intervention narrows or widens disparities.

## Ethics and safeguarding

Obtain informed consent before participation and parental consent plus participant assent for minors. Do not collect passwords, Ghana Card numbers, bank details, one-time PINs, or other unnecessary sensitive information. Pseudonymise participant records, separate consent records from research data, restrict access to authorised researchers, and define retention and deletion periods before collection. Explain that participation is voluntary and that participants may stop without penalty.

## Dissemination

Publish aggregated, de-identified findings. Do not publish raw participant data in this repository. Share practical recommendations with participating communities and relevant Ghanaian institutions in accessible language and formats.
