# Analysis Plan

## Overview

Analyse quantitative and qualitative evidence together to determine whether AI-supported training improves technology literacy and whether effects are equitable across participant groups.

## Quantitative analysis

1. Describe baseline characteristics, AI exposure, access constraints, and scores using counts, percentages, means, medians, and appropriate uncertainty measures.
2. Score the practical assessment from 0 to 12 and construct transparent survey subscales for access, functional skills, critical AI literacy, safety and ethical use, and empowerment.
3. Compare pre-test and post-test changes within each condition.
4. Estimate the intervention effect by comparing change in the intervention group against change in the comparison group. Use regression or mixed-effects models appropriate to the final allocation and repeated-measures structure.
5. Adjust for prespecified baseline covariates where justified, including location, age, gender, education, disability, device access, connectivity, and baseline score.
6. Report subgroup estimates cautiously for urban-rural location, language preference, age, gender, disability, education, and affordability. Distinguish exploratory subgroup findings from confirmatory analyses.
7. Report missing data, attrition, and sensitivity analyses. Do not treat non-response as improvement.

## Qualitative analysis

Transcribe or summarise interviews, focus groups, and observation notes using approved privacy procedures. Use thematic analysis with an initial code set covering access, language, trust, privacy, scams, misinformation, bias, employment, confidence, community support, and overdependence. Permit inductive codes that arise from participant experience. Compare themes across locations and participant groups.

## Integration

Use joint displays or narrative comparison to explain where qualitative evidence supports, qualifies, or contradicts quantitative results. For example, a gain in quiz score without confidence or access may not indicate durable empowerment.

## Reporting

Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not only statistical significance. Present aggregate, de-identified results. Clearly separate observed results from recommendations and limitations, including non-random allocation, short follow-up, device access, connectivity disruption, and self-report bias.
