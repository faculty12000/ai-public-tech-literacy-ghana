# AI Public Technology Literacy Ghana

This repository contains the AfricArXiv preprint and a reusable study package for evaluating whether artificial intelligence improves public technology literacy in Ghana. The work examines access, practical skills, critical evaluation, safety, ethical use, and real-world empowerment.

The proposed study uses a mixed-methods, quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test design. It compares AI-supported digital-literacy training with conventional digital-literacy training and is designed for urban, peri-urban, rural, and low-connectivity Ghanaian communities.

## Repository contents

- `paper/` contains the canonical preprint, *Evaluating AI's Role in Public Technology Literacy in Ghana: A Mixed-Methods Research Protocol and Conceptual Framework*.
- `protocol/` documents the study design, ethics, and implementation flow.
- `instruments/` contains draft survey, practical assessment, and interview materials.
- `methodology/` contains the sampling and analysis plans.
- `references/` contains the references cited in the paper.
- `data/` explains how study data should be handled. No participant-level data is published here.

## Research question

Does AI help Ghanaians become more capable, critical, safe, and independent technology users, or does it mainly make digital services easier to consume without improving understanding?

## Scope

The preprint is a research protocol and conceptual framework; it reports no participant results and has not been peer reviewed. Before field deployment, obtain ethics approval where required, complete a formal sample-size justification and preregistration, translate and pilot-test instruments with intended communities, and adapt safeguarding and referral information to each study location.
